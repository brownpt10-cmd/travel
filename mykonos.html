Mykonos, Greece

Research Report — as of Sep 12, 2026
Overview Getting Around Sights Entertainment Food & Drink Nature Niche Weather YouTube Sources Open Qs

Mykonos is the Cyclades' most famous island — whitewashed Chora lanes, iconic windmills, and a reputation built on beach clubs, nightlife, and a jet-set crowd, but also genuine Cycladic architecture, a walkable old town, and one of the best day trips in Greece (Delos) a short ferry ride away. It's smaller and easier to navigate than it looks online — most of what matters sits within 15km of Mykonos Town.

Your bullseye — Olia Hotel, Tourlos: right at the New Port, the island's main ferry gateway. Great for arrivals/departures, but Mykonos Town itself (Chora, Little Venice, the Windmills) is a short drive, not a walk — see Getting Around.

Getting Around
  • Walking to Mykonos Town — don't. It's only ~2.5km from Tourlos/New Port to Chora, but the road has no proper pavement for most of the way and is widely flagged as unpleasant/unsafe on foot. Use the sea bus (~8 min), the shuttle bus (10–15 min), or a taxi (~5 min) instead.
  • Public bus (KTEL) — hub at Fabrika Square in town; routes to most beaches (Paradise, Platys Gialos, Ornos, Elia, Ano Mera). Cheap, but September schedules thin out — confirm locally.
  • Rental car / ATV / scooter — the practical way to reach north-coast beaches (Panormos, Agios Ioannis, Fokos) and Ano Mera; ~€30–45/day for a car.
  • Taxis — famously scarce on Mykonos; pre-book for anything time-sensitive.
  • Sea taxis / caïques — seasonal boats linking the south-coast beaches (Ornos ↔ Psarou ↔ Platys Gialos ↔ Paranga ↔ Paradise ↔ Super Paradise) — beach-hop without a car.
  • To Delos — ferry from the Old Port in Mykonos Town (not Tourlos), 20–40 min crossing (~30 min typical), morning departures around 9:00/10:00/11:30 Tue–Sun, seasonal through October, €20–25 round trip + €8–12 site admission.
Tourist Spots / Must-See

First Order

Little Venice (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min)

Pastel houses with balconies hanging directly over the sea; the single most-photographed corner of Mykonos, best at sunset.

Windmills / Kato Mili (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min)

Row of 16th-century whitewashed windmills above Little Venice; free, always open, the classic skyline shot.

Panagia Paraportiani (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min)

Unusual sculptural whitewashed church fused from four (some say five) smaller chapels, by the old harbor — one of the most photographed churches in Greece.

Mykonos Town (Chora) old town (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min)

The maze of whitewashed lanes, boutiques, and bougainvillea most people picture when they picture Mykonos.

Delos day trip (ferry from Old Port)

UNESCO World Heritage site, mythological birthplace of Apollo & Artemis — ancient theater, mosaics, Terrace of the Lions, small museum. Realistically a half-day to full-day.

Second Order

  • Ano Mera (drive ~11km) — the island's other village, far quieter/more traditional than Chora, centered on its main square.
  • Panagia Tourliani Monastery (drive ~11km) — 16th-century monastery in Ano Mera, ornate marble bell tower, carved wood iconostasis.
  • Mykonos Archaeological Museum (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min) — Cycladic finds, much excavated from Delos and Rhenia.
  • Aegean Maritime Museum (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min) — ship models, old maps, a reconstructed 19th-c. lighthouse lamp room.
  • Lena's House Folklore Museum (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min) — a preserved 19th-century Mykonian merchant's house.
  • Rarity Gallery (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min) — contemporary art gallery on Kalogera St. in the old town, free to browse.
Entertainment

First Order

  • Little Venice sunset bars (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min) — balconies literally over the water facing the sunset and windmills; Katerina's Bar is the most-cited "best sunset spot," alongside Kastro's next door.
  • Scorpios (Paraga) (drive ~9km) — the island's famous "sunset ritual" beach club/restaurant — DJ sets, design-forward, a scene rather than a quiet drink.
  • Nammos (Psarou) (drive ~7km) — the original Mykonos luxury beach-club-restaurant, a longtime celebrity magnet.
  • Cavo Paradiso (Paradise Beach area) (drive ~8.5km) — the island's marquee late-night superclub, international headline DJs through September.

Second Order

  • Paradise Beach party strip (drive ~8.5km) and Super Paradise Beach (drive ~10km) — daytime beach-club parties running into the evening.
  • Ano Mera taverna evenings (drive ~11km) — a quieter, local alternative to a night in Chora.
Food & Beverage

Signature dishes & drinks — try these

  • Kopanisti — Mykonos's famous spicy, tangy, fermented soft cheese (a P.D.O. product) — the island's most distinctive food export, usually served as a meze.
  • Louza — thin-sliced, salt- and spice-cured, air-dried pork loin — a Mykonian charcuterie specialty.
  • Ksinotiri — another local fermented, sour cheese, firmer than kopanisti; good with tomato, bread, or pasta.
  • Mykonian sausage — lean, oregano-and-spice-seasoned dried pork sausage.
  • Amygdalota — almond cookies scented with rosewater or orange blossom water, dusted in powdered sugar.
  • Soumada — a non-alcoholic almond-and-rosewater drink (distinct from Paros's grape-based souma).
  • Melopita — a local honey-and-soft-cheese pie found in bakeries island-wide.

Nature & Outdoors

First Order

  • Psarou Beach (drive ~7km) — soft golden sand, calm clear water, the island's most upscale/beach-club-dense stretch (Nammos is here).
  • Platys Gialos (drive ~7km) — family-friendly, organized, a hub for sea taxis to the other south-coast beaches.
  • Ornos Beach (drive ~6km) — sheltered bay, calm water, easy bus access.
  • Paradise Beach (drive ~8.5km) — the original party beach, still buzzing with beach clubs and music through September.
  • Super Paradise Beach (drive ~10km) — livelier/younger, split between party and quieter sections; reachable by sea taxi from Platys Gialos.

Second Order

  • Panormos Beach (drive 7.5km) — quieter, more bohemian north-coast cove; less developed, good windsurfing conditions.
  • Agios Ioannis Beach (drive ~7.5km) — small, scenic, west-facing sunset beach (the "Shirley Valentine" beach from the 1989 film).
  • Elia Beach (drive ~14km) — the island's longest organized beach, mixed gay-friendly and family sections; reachable by bus, car, or sea taxi.
  • Kalafatis Beach — windsurfing/diving hub on the east coast. (distance unconfirmed — verify locally)
Niche / Off-the-Beaten-Path
  • Fokos Beach (drive ~15km) — the island's most remote/undeveloped beach, reached via Ano Mera on unpaved roads; no sunbeds, one taverna, genuinely quiet even in season.
  • Armenistis Lighthouse — 19th-century lighthouse on the island's northern tip near Fanari; no bus access, reached by rental car/scooter via Agios Stefanos, or a guided walking tour. (distance unconfirmed — verify locally)
  • Rarity Gallery (drive 2.5km — sea bus 8 min) — contemporary art in the old town, mostly overlooked by beach-and-club visitors.
  • Ano Mera village life (drive ~11km) — cafés on the main square with essentially no tourist crowds, a genuine contrast to Chora.
  • Delos after the tour groups leave (ferry from Old Port) — the last afternoon boat back gives a much quieter window on the ruins than the morning cruise-ship rush.

Multiple sources recommend early September for nightlife/beach-club energy and mid-to-late September for a calmer, better-value trip with the same warm sea. Meltemi winds are a known Cyclades factor — can affect ferries and comfort on exposed north-coast beaches; check forecasts close to travel.

  • Kalafatis Beach and Armenistis Lighthouse distances from the bullseye weren't confidently sourced — left unlabeled; confirm locally or via GPS on arrival.
  • Ano Mera, Fokos, Elia, Agios Ioannis, and Panormos distances are derived by adding Tourlos's own ~2.5km offset from Chora to each beach's published "from Mykonos Town" figure — directional, not turn-by-turn verified.
