Tent Plan — Walk-In Strategy

No reservation, two weekday windows

Party of 3 is too small for a table reservation this close to the date — those go in blocks of 8–10, months out. Both tent days (Tue 9/22, Wed 9/23) are weekdays — the easiest walk-in window of the festival. By regulation, large tents hold back at least 25% of seats for walk-ins at all times.

  • Best targets: Augustiner-Festhalle (locals arrive late), Schützen-Festzelt (remote, lighter crowds), Hacker-Festzelt (~2,600 walk-in seats even on weekends).
  • Also workable: Paulaner Festzelt, Festhalle Schottenhamel, Hofbräu-Festzelt (standing-room section always available), Armbrustschützenzelt, Löwenbräu-Festzelt, Festzelt Pschorr Bräurosl, Ochsenbraterei, Fischer-Vroni.
  • 21 small tents (Straße 1–5) never take reservations — always first-come, good for a change of pace or specific food.
  • Plan: grounds by 9:00–9:30 AM, at the first tent's door by 9:45, seated at 10:00 open, then hop to a 2nd/3rd tent or beer garden through the afternoon.
  • Bench seating is communal — ask "Ist hier noch frei?" ("Is this still free?") before sitting.
  • A Maß (1 liter) runs roughly €15–16. Cash matters — many stalls are cash-preferred.
Skip: Marstall-Festzelt, Kufflers Weinzelt, Käfer Wiesn-Schänke — near-full reservation tents with little walk-in room.

Food & Dining

Hofbräuhaus

Am Platzl 9, Munich. Dinner planned Mon 9/21 around 5:00 PM. Classic beer hall — no reservation needed for a party this size, but arrive early for a table.

Viktualienmarkt

Munich's year-round food market near Marienplatz — good stop between the Glockenspiel and St. Peter's tower on Tuesday. Cash-friendly stalls, bratwurst, produce, beer garden in the center.

Gasthaus zur Schranne — Garmisch

Historic inn, locals' favorite. Lunch stop ~1:15 PM on the Wednesday day trip after Partnachklamm. Schnitzel, potato dumplings, beer garden if weather holds. ~€20–30/person.

Small tents at Oktoberfest

21 small tents around the grounds specialize in roast chicken, schnitzel, fish, or cocktails/café fare — good hopping stops between big-tent sessions, and always walk-in.

Sights & Entertainment

Glockenspiel

Marienplatz — figures perform at 11 AM and noon (also 5 PM in summer). Tue 9/22 morning stop.

St. Peter's Church Tower

Climb for the best rooftop view in the city — pairs well with the Glockenspiel and Viktualienmarkt on Tuesday.

English Garden & Eisbach Surfers

One of the world's largest urban parks — watch surfers ride the standing wave on the Eisbach. Suggested for the last morning, Thu 9/24.

The Residenz

Former royal palace of the Wittelsbach monarchs, central Munich — alternative to the English Garden for Thursday morning if the weather's poor.

Zugspitze & Partnachklamm

Germany's highest peak (2,962m) and a dramatic river gorge near Garmisch — full detail and hour-by-hour timing on the Wednesday itinerary page.

Special Directions & Notes

Garmisch packing list: warm layer/windbreaker (summit is cold and windy even in September), light rain shell (Partnachklamm path drips throughout), sturdy grippy shoes, sunglasses (glare off the glacier), cash or card for gorge ticket machines (no mobile signal at the entrance).

Garmisch tickets: Bayern-Ticket (~€29, covers S-Bahn + regional train + local buses) and Zugspitze Round Trip ticket (~€60–80, separate — book online for a timed slot) — the Bayern-Ticket does not cover the Zugspitzbahn. Partnachklamm entrance ~€10.

Weather call: if the forecast looks poor for the Zugspitze summit, the gorge and lunch alone make for a much more relaxed day — check the Zugspitze webcam/forecast that morning.
