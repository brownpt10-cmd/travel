Start to finish on foot: out of the hotel, over the Ludwigsbrücke, through Isartor into the old town, and back the same way. Straightforward, and it uses most of the 3-mile budget just getting there and back — this is the option if you'd rather not deal with a tram ticket before coffee.

Out the door and onto Rosenheimer Str. toward the Ludwigsbrücke. This is the only genuinely dull stretch of either route — it's over in 10 minutes.

Isartor
0.55 mi · ~11 minfrom hotel
One of three surviving gates from Munich's 14th-century city wall (1337), with a 19th-century fresco over the arch depicting Emperor Ludwig the Bavarian's victory procession. You'll cross the Isar on the Ludwigsbrücke to get here — worth a glance upstream at the Deutsches Museum on its own island.
3
Marienplatz
0.35 mi · ~7 minvia Tal
Munich's central square since the 1100s. The Neo-Gothic Neues Rathaus dominates the north side; the gilded Mariensäule column (1638) marks the center, raised to mark the end of Swedish occupation in the Thirty Years' War.Glockenspiel doesn't perform until 11 AM — you'll have the square to admire, not the show.
4
Frauenkirche
0.2 mi round trip · ~4 minshort detour, back to Marienplatz
The cathedral's twin onion domes (1524–25) are Munich's skyline signature. Worth the two-minute walk over just to stand under them — no tower climb needed.
5
Coffee — Rischart Café am Marienplatz
right on the square~20 min break
Bakery-café at Marienplatz 18, with a view over the action. If the sit-down café isn't open yet, the bakery counter next to it usually is — coffee and a pastry to go works just as well.Confirm hours the morning of — cafés here tend to open around 7–8 AM, not earlier.
6
Viktualienmarkt
0.15 mi · ~3 minfrom Marienplatz
Munich's permanent open-air market since 1807, built around a maypole. This early it's stallholders setting up rather than tourist crowds — a good slow lap with coffee in hand.
7
Asam Church
0.4 mi round trip · ~8 mindetour down Sendlinger Str.
A tiny, densely ornamented Baroque church (1733–46) that the Asam brothers built onto their own house next door — one of the city's most over-the-top interiors squeezed behind a facade barely wider than a doorway. Step inside; it's free and takes five minutes.
8
Coffee — Café Frischhut ("Die Schmalznudel")
0.1 mi · ~2 min~20 min break
A Munich original near the market, famous for one thing: the Schmalznudel, a flat fried-dough pastry served warm. This is deliberately your last stop, not your first.Opens 9:00 AM on weekdays — timed to land here right around then.

Retrace the outbound path. Expect to be back around 9:30–9:45 AM if you left at 7:30.

Before you go

Ankle-friendly by design

Both routes stay under 3 miles and skip every tower climb (Frauenkirche, St. Peter's, Neues Rathaus) — just street level. Old-town cobblestones are mostly flat but uneven in patches; the 20-min-mile pace has slack built in, so there's no need to push through it.

Hours are estimates

Café Frischhut's 9:00 AM weekday opening is well documented; Rischart's café hours are less certain online — the bakery counter tends to open earlier than the sit-down café. Worth a quick check the morning you go.

Weather

Late-September mornings in Munich run cool, often near 50°F/10°C before the sun's up. Layer for the walk over.

Tickets

A single MVV ticket (or the MVGO app) covers the tram or S-Bahn hop in Option B — no need for a day pass just for one ride.

Distances and times are route estimates for planning, not measured GPS tracks — treat clock times as a guide, not a schedule. Café hours current as of Sept 2026 research; reconfirm close to travel.
